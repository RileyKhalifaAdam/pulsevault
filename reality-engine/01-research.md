# Reality engine — research findings

Web-verified research sweeps (2026-11-23). "unverified" = from memory, not checked against a source.

## Continuum and particle methods for simulating matter in real time (MPM family, SPH, PBF/PBD, real-time FEM, fracture, phase change, unified frameworks) and their fit to a base iPhone 15 (A16)

Verification caveat first: direct page fetch was blocked in this session (arxiv.org and www.math.ucla.edu are outside the allowed network domains and would have to be allowed in the session's network settings; the GitHub tool is restricted to the user's repo). Every item marked verified=true was confirmed only through WebSearch extracts quoting the cited source, not a full-page read; derived arithmetic and memory items are marked verified=false. Several wanted numbers could not be retrieved (Fei 2021 single-GPU per-substep timings, Flex Table 1, XPBI timings, CD-MPM timings).

Conclusion: no single discretization is a credible sole substrate for matter at real stiffness on a phone-class GPU. The binding constraint is wave speed, not particle count: an explicit solver at 1 cm resolution would need roughly 2,500 (water) to 9,800 (steel) substeps per 60 Hz frame, against the 2-4 that real-time demos budget. All real-time systems cope by one of three routes: softening moduli by orders of magnitude, implicit or position-based solves whose effective stiffness depends on iteration budget, or idealizing stiff matter as rigid.

The most credible design is two-tier, unified by one SI material and constitutive library rather than one solver:
- Flowing and breaking matter (liquids, granular, snow, mud, soft and plastic solids, melting): MLS-MPM/APIC, ideally in a time-step-robust form (PB-MPM-style or implicit), because it needs no neighbor search, handles topology change and self-collision for free, and takes the widest range of constitutive models.
- Stiff structural matter and mechanisms: rigid bodies plus an implicit constraint/FEM solver (AVBD/VBD/XPBD family), coupled to MPM through explicit frictional contact.
State-of-the-art "unified" engines in 2026 (Newton 1.x, Genesis) are themselves multi-solver, which supports this.

Phone-scale sizing is an estimate, not a measurement: no iPhone MPM benchmark exists in the literature found. Anchors are a WebGPU MLS-MPM demo at about 100k particles on a laptop integrated GPU at 2 steps per frame, and a vendor statement of "tens of thousands of particles on mobile". A planning figure of about 50-100k MPM particles at 60 Hz implies dx of 2-5 cm, an active window of 64^3 to 128^3 cells (1.3-2.6 m cube at 2 cm; 3.2-6.4 m at 5 cm), and only about 0.1 m^3 of actual matter at 2 cm (about 1.5 m^3 at 5 cm) at 8 particles per cell. That is a tabletop-to-room-corner sandbox; everything else must be rigid, sleeping or analytic.

### Findings
- Derived CFL bound (dt <= dx/c): with longitudinal sound speed about 5,900 m/s in steel and 1,482 m/s in water, a 1 cm grid needs dt <= 1.7 microseconds (steel) or 6.7 microseconds (water), i.e. at least ~9,800 or ~2,500 explicit substeps per 60 Hz frame before any safety factor. Real-time demos budget 2-4 substeps. — [high, unverified] Derived; sound speeds from https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/sound/souspe2.html and https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/sound-speed-solids-d_713.html (search extracts)
- Fei, Huang, Gao 2021 (the reference real-time GPU MPM paper) reports a 1.33M-particle snow scene at 68.5 fps and a 143K-particle fountain at 55.9 fps on four Tesla V100 GPUs with NVLink, not one GPU, plus 1.7-8.6x single-GPU speedup over prior art. The smaller fluid scene being slower than the larger snow scene suggests substep count, not particle count, dominates cost (my inference). — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00699 (search extract; direct fetch blocked)
- A WebGPU MLS-MPM fluid demo (matsuoka-601 WebGPU-Ocean, Jan-Feb 2025) runs about 100k particles in real time on a Ryzen 7 5825U integrated GPU, about 300k on mid-range GPUs and 500k on an RTX 3060 Laptop, at 2 simulation steps per frame with atomicAdd-based P2G. The same author's SPH version reached only about 30k particles on the iGPU because neighbor search was the bottleneck. — [medium] https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/26/webgpu-fluid-simulations-high-performance-real-time-rendering/ ; https://80.lv/articles/check-out-this-real-time-3d-fluid-simulation-implemented-in-webgpu ; https://cgworld.jp/flashnews/202501-matsuoka-601-LiquidSim.html (search extracts)
- No published MPM benchmark on an iPhone or A16 was found. The closest mobile data point is Zibra (MLS-MPM/APIC-based, supports iOS Metal arm64) stating "tens of thousands of particles on mobile devices" versus hundreds of thousands on laptops; the A16 5-core GPU scores about 2,900-3,360 in 3DMark Wild Life Extreme. — [medium] https://www.zibra.ai/blog-posts/approaches-to-real-time-fluid-simulation-in-visual-effects ; https://assetstore-fallback.unity.com/packages/tools/physics/zibra-liquid-266451 ; https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-A16-GPU-5-Core-GPU-Benchmarks-and-Specs.652746.0.html (search extracts)
- Current "unified" engines are multi-solver, not single-substrate. Newton (1.0 GA at GTC March 2026, v1.5.0 in Aug 2026, Linux Foundation since 2025-09-29, Apache-2.0, requires warp-lang>=1.16) ships XPBD, VBD, semi-implicit, implicit MPM and MuJoCo Warp solvers, with VBD and MPM coupled to MuJoCo Warp explicitly; Genesis lists separate Rigid, MPM, SPH, FEM, PBD and Stable Fluid solvers. — [high] https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/newton-adds-contact-rich-manipulation-and-locomotion-capabilities-for-industrial-robotics ; https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/releases ; https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-contribution-of-newton-by-disney-research-google-deepmind-and-nvidia-to-accelerate-open-robot-learning ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world/v0.4.7/README.md (search extracts)
- PB-MPM (Lewin, EA SEED, SIGGRAPH 2024 Talk) is a semi-implicit compliant-constraint MPM described as "stable at any time-step", with one Jacobi-style iteration costing about one explicit MPM step, covering liquids, elastic solids, sand and visco-plastics. The public code is 2D WebGPU (BSD-3; EA's 3D version is in-house CPU), and a later paper criticizes it for PBD-style non-physical parameters and iteration-dependent stiffness. — [medium] https://www.ea.com/seed/news/siggraph2024-pbmpm ; https://github.com/electronicarts/pbmpm ; https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18437v1 (search extracts)
- MLS-MPM (Hu et al. 2018) gives about a 2x speedup over traditional MPM with almost identical visual results and derives APIC/PolyPIC from one discretization. Its CPIC extension adds cutting, thin boundaries and two-way rigid-body coupling. — [high] https://www.math.ucla.edu/~cffjiang/research/mlsmpm/hu2018mlsmpm.pdf ; https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3197517.3201293 (search extracts)
- Particle-grid transfer choice sets the dissipation artifact: PIC dissipates angular momentum severely, FLIP keeps energy but is noisy, and APIC conserves linear and angular momentum with FLIP-like dissipation and no noise. PolyPIC generalizes APIC to polynomial modes for better energy and vorticity conservation. — [high] https://www.math.ucla.edu/~cffjiang/research/apic/paper.pdf ; https://www.math.ucla.edu/~cffjiang/research/polypic/fu2017ppic.pdf (search extracts)
- Explicit MPM stability is not guaranteed by the CFL condition alone. Sun et al. 2020 note graphics time steps are usually chosen by trial and error and that a splashing-fluid case needed an extra single-particle restriction; Bai and Schroeder 2022 find boundaries less stable than interiors and pathological growth with APIC/CPIC at arbitrarily small time steps. — [high] https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~craigs/papers/2020-sound-speed/paper.pdf ; https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14620 (search extracts)
- Documented non-physical MPM artifacts: cell-crossing instability from discontinuous linear shape-function gradients (mitigated by GIMP, CPDI or B-spline kernels), numerical fracture when particles separate beyond kernel support (CPDI does not fully remove it), and volumetric locking near incompressibility (needs an F-bar-type fix; higher-order bases alone do not cure it). — [high] https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10338v1 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00388 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02466v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03826v1 (search extracts)
- MPM's built-in contact is sticky: single-velocity-field MPM prevents interpenetration for free but only models stick contact with no tangential slip, and suffers premature contact detection at grid-cell distance. Frictional sliding needs multi-field MPM or an explicit coupling scheme. — [high] https://www.cof.orst.edu/cof/wse/faculty/Nairn/papers/MPMContactRevisited.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13534v1 (search extracts)
- Offline-scale GPU MPM references: Gao 2018 ran 4.2M particles on a 256^3 grid with implicit dynamics and heat at 10.5 s per 48 Hz frame on a Quadro P6000; Wang 2020 ran about 100M particles on 1024^3 in under 4 min/frame on 4 GPUs (110-120x per-step over SIMD CPU). QuanTaichi fit 235M particles on one GPU through quantization. — [high] https://people.csail.mit.edu/kuiwu/GPU_MPM/GPU_MPM.pdf ; https://yzhu.io/publication/mpmgpu2020siggraph/paper.pdf ; https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/143474 (search extracts)
- Recent MPM work: CK-MPM (TOG 2025) uses a C2 compact kernel on a dual grid for about 2x faster G2P2G than quadratic B-splines but tends to promote fracture; MPM Lite (arXiv Feb 2026) uses linear kernels for 1.69x explicit and up to 15.9x implicit speedup at 24 particles per cell. A May 2026 sparse framework uses GPU hashing for 1-2 orders of magnitude savings in strongly sparse scenes, and "Substepping the MPM" (Aug 2025) wraps explicit substeps into a pseudo-implicit macro step. — [medium] https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10399 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07853 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28525v1 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11722 (search extracts)
- Implicit MPM is the published answer to stiffness: HOT (Wang 2020) states explicit MPM needs time steps far below CFL for stiff materials such as metal and is up to 10x faster than prior implicit MPM codes. Newton's SolverImplicitMPM (after Daviet and Bertails-Descoubes 2016, Drucker-Prager rheology) is documented as unconditionally stable in dt and best for very stiff or fully inelastic materials, but less versatile than explicit MPM, with a default of 250 rheology iterations. — [medium] https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07913 ; https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/api/_generated/newton.solvers.SolverImplicitMPM.html (search extracts)
- Rigid-MPM coupling precedent for mechanisms acting on matter: the Drake convex formulation (Yu et al. 2025) integrates MPM explicitly at small steps on GPU and rigid bodies at larger steps via asynchronous time-splitting, reporting up to 500x speedup over the earlier convex formulation. It ships in open-source Drake. — [medium] https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05046 (search extract)
- Vertex Block Descent (SIGGRAPH 2024) is an unconditionally stable block-coordinate descent on implicit Euler; its largest scene (48M vertices, 151M tetrahedra) takes 3.6 s per step, i.e. offline. The authors report poor convergence at a 1:10000 stiffness ratio and call it not ideal for high-resolution stiff systems. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06321 ; https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/vbd/ (search extracts)
- Augmented VBD (Giles, Diaz, Yuksel, SIGGRAPH 2025) adds augmented-Lagrangian hard constraints and simulates 110,000 rigid blocks in 3.5 ms (9.8 ms with collision detection) and 510,000 in 10.3 ms (17.6 ms) per frame on an RTX 4090 with 4 iterations. This is the strongest published real-time result for stiff bodies and joints. — [high] https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf ; https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25_RTL.pdf (search extracts)
- XPBD replaces PBD's iteration- and time-step-dependent stiffness with a compliance (inverse stiffness in physical units), but it only matches the physical answer at convergence. "Small Steps" (Macklin 2019) shows n substeps of 1 iteration beat 1 step of n iterations (30x1 at 13.5 ms was visibly stiffer than 1x30 at 12.4 ms). — [medium] https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf ; https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1145/3309486-3340247 (search extracts; timing example from an unofficial copy)
- Constitutive coverage differs by solver family: stable Neo-Hookean (Smith 2018) handles Poisson ratio near 0.5, inversion and rest stability, and Macklin and Mueller 2021 reproduce it in XPBD as deviatoric plus hydrostatic constraints. Projective Dynamics does not support Neo-Hookean or Mooney-Rivlin (Liu 2017), and XPBD is limited to energies quadratic in a constraint. — [medium] https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StableElasticity/paper.pdf ; https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/neohookean.pdf ; https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/liu16towards/liu16towards.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23131v1 (search extracts)
- Unified Particle Physics / Flex (Macklin 2014, SIGGRAPH 2025 Test-of-Time award) ran real time on a GTX 680 at 16 ms frames, with a 300k-particle Rayleigh-Taylor scene at about 150 ms per frame. Its rigid bodies use shape matching, whose cost grows with particle count, propagates impulses slowly and handles joints poorly; Position Based Fluids explicitly trades incompressibility for speed. — [medium] https://mmacklin.com/uppfrta_preprint.pdf ; https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf ; https://cgweb.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/intern/seminar/positionBasedFluids_2014_SIGGRAPH.pdf (search extracts)
- Incompressible SPH is accurate but offline at scale: DFSPH holds volume compression below 0.01% with over 20x speedup against then-current SPH (5M fluid plus 40M boundary particles at 5 s per step), and IISPH ran 30M particles at 44 s per step with dt = 4 ms and under 0.1% compression on a 16-core PC. Weakly compressible SPH instead uses a Tait equation of state with an artificial sound speed to trade a Poisson solve for small user-defined density fluctuations. — [high] https://animation.rwth-aachen.de/media/papers/2015-SCA-DFSPH.pdf ; https://cg.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/publications/2013_TVCG_IISPH.pdf ; https://cgweb.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/publications/2007_SCA_SPH.pdf (search extracts)
- First-principles fracture is not demonstrated in real time: CD-MPM (Wolper 2019) offers a phase-field model needing a coupled momentum/damage solve and a Non-Associated Cam-Clay plasticity model, shown at 7.79M particles. Peridynamics in graphics (Levine 2014, Chen 2018) is a trivially parallel explicit spring-mass-like scheme with a von-Neumann-bounded time step; no mobile or real-time results were found for either. — [medium] https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10108350 ; https://cal.cs.umbc.edu/Papers/Levine-2014-APP/Levine-2014-APP.pdf ; https://www.sc.fsu.edu/images/stories/xpo/2012/steve-henke.pdf (search extracts)
- Phase change from first principles exists for MPM: Augmented MPM (Stomakhin 2014) solves the heat equation rho*c*DT/Dt = div(kappa*grad T) implicitly on the grid, tracks latent heat L in a per-particle buffer, varies Young's modulus and Poisson ratio with temperature and phase, and adds a MAC-grid Chorin projection for incompressibility. Gao 2018's 4.2M-particle melting demo used this class of solver at 10.5 s per frame. — [high] https://www.math.ucla.edu/~cffjiang/research/melt/paper.pdf ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US10210287 ; https://people.csail.mit.edu/kuiwu/GPU_MPM/GPU_MPM.pdf (search extracts)
- Plasticity models consume few measurable parameters: the Stomakhin 2013 snow model uses critical compression 2.5e-2, critical stretch 7.5e-3 and hardening coefficient 10; Drucker-Prager sand (Klar 2016, Hencky-strain elasticity) takes the friction angle phi through alpha = sqrt(2/3)*2*sin(phi)/(3 - sin(phi)). — [medium] https://osupdocs.forestry.oregonstate.edu/index.php/Clamped_Neo-Hookean_Material ; https://www.math.ucla.edu/~cffjiang/research/sand/paper.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11694 (search extracts, partly third-party restatements)
- From memory: the snow paper's elastic parameters are E0 = 1.4e5 Pa, density 400 kg/m^3 and Poisson ratio 0.2. The full parameter set a first-principles material library needs is density, Young's modulus and Poisson ratio (Lame parameters), von Mises yield stress and hardening, friction angle and cohesion, bulk modulus and Tait exponent (gamma = 7 for water), dynamic viscosity (Herschel-Bulkley yield stress, consistency and flow index for non-Newtonian fluids), fracture energy or critical stretch, and conductivity, specific heat and latent heat. — [medium, unverified] memory (could not open https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/SSCTS13.pdf)
- Typical real-time MPM demos use non-SI softened moduli tuned to sit at the CFL limit: Taichi's mpm128 example uses 9,000 particles on a 128^2 grid in a unit domain with E = 5e3, nu = 0.2 and dt = 1e-4 (the 88-line variant uses E = 400, dt = 2e-4, 8,192 particles). — [high] https://github.com/taichi-dev/taichi/blob/4f5cc09ae0e35a47ad71fdc582c1ecd5202114d8/examples/mpm128.py (search extract)
- Derived stiffness ceiling at a phone budget: with dx = 2 cm and 4 explicit substeps per 60 Hz frame, wave speed must stay below about 4.8 m/s, i.e. a P-wave modulus of about 23 kPa at 1000 kg/m^3, roughly 10^5 times softer than water's 2.2 GPa bulk modulus. The published snow parameters would need at least ~16 substeps per frame at that resolution. — [high, unverified] Derived from the CFL condition and the parameters above; water bulk modulus consistent with https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/sound/souspe2.html
- Genesis's headline speed (43M FPS for a Franka arm on an RTX 4090) was disputed: an independent test with realistic settings measured about 0.29M FPS (150x lower) and found it 3-10x slower than ManiSkill on manipulation. Its README lists CPU, Nvidia/AMD GPU and Apple Metal backends. — [medium] https://stoneztao.substack.com/p/the-new-hyped-genesis-simulator-is ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world/v0.4.7/README.md (search extracts)
- Taichi AOT is a documented route for compiled simulation kernels on mobile (official Android/Vulkan walkthrough with an implicit FEM demo, a Metal backend, OPPO wallpapers shipped via ti.aot.Module), but no phone MPM benchmark was found. — [medium] https://docs.taichi-lang.org/blog/taichi-aot-the-solution-for-deploying-kernels-in-mobile-devices ; https://docs.taichi-lang.org/user-stories/oppo-user-story (search extracts)
- XPBI (Yu et al. 2024) brings updated-Lagrangian elastoplasticity (sand, snow, plasticine) into XPBD alongside cloth and water, but its authors state the grid-colored Gauss-Seidel solver performs poorly on small cases on GPUs and that a tailored real-time solver is future work. — [medium] https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11694 (search extract)

### Implications for the design
- Do not promise one solver for all matter. Plan a two-tier substrate unified by one SI material database and constitutive library: an MLS-MPM/APIC tier for matter that flows, piles, tears or melts, and a rigid plus implicit-constraint tier (AVBD/VBD/XPBD family) for stiff structural matter and mechanisms, which is where the "robot on top of reality" parts live.
- State the wave-speed compromise explicitly as the one sanctioned departure from real parameters. Keep density, gravity, friction angle, yield and strength, viscosity and thermal properties real; cap elastic wave speed (the standard rule from memory is sound speed about 10x the maximum flow speed for about 1% density error); treat truly stiff solids as rigid or as implicit compliance.
- Softening a modulus while keeping a real yield stress changes the yield strain, so the material library needs a defined scaling rule that preserves the visible dimensionless groups (Froude number, friction angle, strength over rho*g*L) and relaxes the invisible ones (Mach number).
- Plan around roughly 50-100k MPM particles with 2-4 substeps or a handful of implicit iterations per 60 Hz frame, and treat this as an unmeasured estimate. Memory is not the constraint (about 100 bytes per particle plus a 128^3 grid is tens of MB); GPU throughput and thermal throttling are.
- Design the world as a small materialized MPM window (64^3 dense to 128^3 sparse cells at dx 2-5 cm) inside a larger rigid, sleeping or analytic world, with matter promoted into MPM near interactions and demoted afterwards. At 8 particles per cell, 100k particles is only about 0.1 m^3 of matter at 2 cm.
- Prefer a time-step-robust MPM formulation (PB-MPM-style constraints, implicit MPM, or a pressure projection for liquids) over pure explicit integration, since explicit weakly compressible water at phone budgets will be visibly compressible. The cost is iteration-dependent effective stiffness, which must be measured and reported, not hidden.
- Use APIC/MLS-MPM transfers and quadratic B-splines as the baseline to avoid PIC dissipation and cell-crossing, add an F-bar-type fix for near-incompressible materials, and evaluate compact or linear kernels (CK-MPM, MPM Lite) for mobile cost with the known risk of extra numerical fracture.
- Mechanism-on-matter interaction needs an explicit two-way rigid-MPM coupling with Coulomb friction (CPIC or Drake-style asynchronous coupling), because native MPM contact is sticky and acts at grid-cell distance.
- Fracture on the phone should come from plasticity with softening or critical-stretch damage in the MPM tier and energy-criterion breakage in the stiff tier. Phase-field and peridynamic fracture have no real-time evidence, and MPM fracture is resolution-dependent unless regularized.
- Newton/Warp, Genesis and Flex are reference implementations and validation oracles for the Linux dev box, not shippable phone runtimes; the phone solver will have to be hand-written GPU compute. The only quantitative small-GPU data found comes from WebGPU implementations (WebGPU-Ocean, PB-MPM), which also suits a Linux-only dev environment.
- Build a first-principles validation suite early: mass, momentum and angular momentum conservation, energy drift, hydrostatic pressure, dam break, granular column collapse versus friction angle, beam deflection versus Euler-Bernoulli, Stokes terminal velocity. Numerical dissipation at cm resolution means real water Reynolds numbers are unreachable, so fine splash and turbulence detail is lower fidelity by construction.

### Open questions
- What sustained MLS-MPM throughput (particles x substeps per second) does a base iPhone 15 deliver under thermal throttling, in Metal compute and in Safari WebGPU? No published figure exists; this needs a week-one prototype.
- Is scatter-style P2G with atomics or a gather-style formulation faster on Apple's GPU, and does either give run-to-run determinism for replays?
- Does a PB-MPM-style solver converge acceptably in 3D with 4-8 iterations per frame? A later paper reports a predecessor failing to converge in 3D with few Jacobi iterations, and EA's 3D version is CPU-only and unpublished.
- How stable and accurate is two-way coupling between an AVBD/XPBD rigid tier at frame rate and substepped MPM, particularly friction, stacking and stickiness at cm resolution?
- What does an incompressible pressure solve cost on a 64^3-128^3 grid on the A16, compared with constraint-based or weakly compressible liquids?
- Which modulus-softening rule is perceptually and physically acceptable, and at what wave-speed cap do players notice bounciness or compressibility?
- Can a resolution-independent fracture criterion (fracture-energy regularized) work at dx = 2-5 cm without an extra phase-field solve?
- Is a hashed sparse grid worth it on Metal/WebGPU at this scale, or does a dense moving 64^3-128^3 window win?
- Unretrieved numbers that would sharpen the scaling estimate: Fei 2021 single-GPU per-substep timings, Young's modulus and dt; Flex Table 1; XPBI and CD-MPM timings. arxiv.org and www.math.ucla.edu need to be allowed for direct reads.
- Can Warp or Taichi AOT target iOS in late 2026? Warp being CUDA/CPU-only and Taichi's maintenance status are from memory and unverified this session.

## Real compute budget of a base iPhone 15 (A16 Bionic, 6 GB, 60 Hz) for a real-time first-principles physics engine

The base iPhone 15 is a 2 P-core + 4 E-core NEON-only CPU with a 5-core Apple8-family GPU (Metal 3 and Metal 4, fp32 only, float atomics yes, 64-bit atomics no), 6 GB LPDDR5 and a 60 Hz panel. Thermals, not peak speed or memory, set the budget: published stress tests put sustained GPU output at 50-85% of peak, and an A16 phone held about 4.3 W over a 30-minute game run.

Defensible per-frame budget at 60 Hz (16.67 ms), measured on a cool device (my derivation, not a published figure):
- GPU: at most ~10 ms total per frame, split as ~4-5 ms physics compute and ~5-6 ms rendering, so the frame still fits when the GPU throttles to ~60%.
- CPU: at most ~4 ms wall for physics on the 2 P-cores (about 6 P-core-ms), with the main thread under ~8 ms.
- Power: about 4 W average device draw, with thermalState staying at or below "fair" over a 20-30 minute soak.
- Fallback: 30 Hz rendering or half-speed simulation when thermalState reaches "serious".

What that buys, as estimates:
- MLS-MPM particles: roughly 10-17k sustained and 15-28k cool, with a quality screen-space fluid renderer. This is scaled from a native Metal app measured on an iPhone 18 Pro Max, and that app's tiers are ones that hold, not maxima. The optimistic ceiling is 50-100k with cheap rendering and 1-2 substeps, scaled from a WebGPU demo on a laptop iGPU.
- 2D rigid bodies: 5,050 stacked bodies cost 0.7-1.4 ms per step and 22,000 cost 2.8-4.9 ms on 1-2 M2 P-cores, which match A16 P-cores in single-thread speed.
- 3D rigid bodies: roughly 1,500-3,000 active stacked bodies in 4 ms. This is a rough estimate from a benchmark on unstated hardware.

Verification caveat: only developer.apple.com and raw.githubusercontent.com could be opened from this session. Findings marked verified=true come from those (Apple Metal tables dated 2026-10-23, the MSL 4.1 spec, Apple docs, forum threads and WWDC pages, LLVM, and project READMEs and benchmark CSVs). Everything from Wikipedia, UL, Notebookcheck, GSMArena, Geekbench and Codrops was seen only as search-engine excerpts and is marked verified=false; those hosts would need to be allowed in the session's network settings to open them directly. No published measurement of MPM, PBF or XPBD throughput on A15-A17 hardware was found.

### Findings
- A16 Bionic is GPU family Apple8 and supports both the Metal 3 and Metal 4 programming models; Metal 4 is available from Apple7 (A14) up. Apple8 has mesh shading, ray tracing, sparse textures, placement sparse buffers and textures (Metal 4), MetalFX temporal upscaling and floating-point atomics, but not 64-bit atomics on iOS (Apple9, A17 Pro and later). — [high] https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Feature-Set-Tables.pdf (dated 2026-10-23); https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/205/
- Apple8 GPU limits: 1024 threads per threadgroup (theoretical; check maxTotalThreadsPerThreadgroup at runtime), 32 KB threadgroup memory, 31 buffer bindings per kernel, 16,384 px max 2D texture, 2,048 px max 3D texture per axis, 256M px texture-buffer width. MTLDevice.maxBufferLength is runtime-only; Apple documents only a 256 MB floor. — [high] https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Feature-Set-Tables.pdf; https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtldevice/maxbufferlength
- There is no fp64 on the GPU: the MSL 4.1 spec (2026-06-04) says Metal does not support the double type. atomic<float> works in device memory from Metal 3 (so on A16), and atomic float add/sub in threadgroup memory arrives only in Metal 4.1; 64-bit integer math is available from Apple3. — [high] https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Shading-Language-Specification.pdf
- A16 CPU SIMD is 128-bit NEON only. ### Implications for the design
- Thermals are the binding constraint, not peak speed or memory. Size every workload to about 60% of what a cool device can do, and build a thermal governor into the engine core that reads ProcessInfo.thermalState and measured GPU time.
- The governor should change resolution, particle count or render rate, never the physical laws: keep a fixed physics timestep decoupled from rendering so behaviour stays deterministic when quality scales.
- The invariant to budget is particle-substeps per frame. With roughly 4-5 ms of GPU compute, expect on the order of 10-30k MLS-MPM particles sustained with a good renderer, and that number falls linearly with substep count.
- Explicit integration of truly stiff materials is out of reach. Steel's sound speed of about 5 km/s at 1 cm resolution needs microsecond timesteps, thousands per frame, so 'first principles' has to mean a multi-model design: rigid or constraint-based solids for stiff matter, MPM or similar for fluids, granular and soft matter, with implicit or position-based solvers where needed.
- All GPU state must be fp32, since Metal has no double. Large worlds and SI units need local origins or scaled units, with any fp64 work kept on the CPU.
- For particle-to-grid transfer on this tile-based GPU, plan on sort-plus-gather as the primary path and float-atomic scatter as a fallback. 64-bit atomics are unavailable on A16.
- CPU design should assume 2 heavy threads at most, with the 4 E-cores for background and low-priority jobs. Write SIMD as 4-wide float NEON; skipping SIMD costs about 3.5x in the Box2D data.
- A CPU rigid-body solver is affordable: a few thousand active 2D bodies cost about 1 ms on a P-core. 3D is likely limited to low thousands of active stacked bodies in 4 ms, so sleeping and islanding are mandatory.
- Settled matter must stop simulating. The one measured iPhone MPM app sleeps resting liquid and drops to 30 Hz, and this is as much a thermal feature as a performance one.
- Memory is not the limiting factor at these scales: 100k particles at 64-96 bytes is under 10 MB and a 128-cubed grid at 16 bytes per cell is about 34 MB. Keep the total footprint under about 2 GB, which is safe under every measured iPhone limit, and read os_proc_available_memory at launch.
- Delivery path matters for the budget. With no Mac attached, WebGPU in Safari on iOS 26 is the route that works from a Linux container and fits the existing PWA repo, but it brings 256 MiB buffers, 128 MiB storage bindings, 8 storage buffers per stage, integer-only atomics, a roughly 2 GB web-process limit and 30 fps in Low Power Mode.
- A native Metal build removes those limits and gives float atomics, 1024-thread groups and Metal 4, but needs a macOS build and signing step (for example cloud macOS CI). An engine core that targets both WebGPU and Metal keeps that option open.
- Metal 4 is usable on A16, so the engine may adopt it, but nothing found suggests it raises compute throughput; treat it as optional.

## Build and delivery path (Linux cloud container to base iPhone 15), language/GPU API choice, and floating-point determinism — state as of 2026-11-23

Ranked recommendation.

1. First on-device demo, least friction: ship the engine as a static web app. Compile a portable core to wasm32, run GPU kernels as WGSL compute through Safari's WebGPU, and serve it from GitHub Pages; the user opens the URL on the iPhone 15 and adds it to the Home Screen. This needs no Mac, no Apple account and no signing, and the whole loop runs from the Linux container with a git push. WebGPU is default-on from Safari 26 (iOS 26), which the iPhone 15 runs. Start single-threaded WASM SIMD plus GPU compute; add threads later through a COOP/COEP service-worker shim.

2. Path that does not cap long-term performance: the same core built as a native iOS app (Rust + wgpu on Metal, with room for hand-written Metal 3/4 kernels), built and signed on GitHub Actions macOS runners and delivered through TestFlight. This works without owning a Mac but needs the paid Apple Developer Program (US$99/yr). It removes the browser's roughly 2 GB process kill limit, the 4-thread cap, the missing haptics and the WebKit shader-compiler quirks.

3. Swift + Metal only: highest ceiling, but it cannot be compiled or tested in the Linux container and gives no web demo, so it is a later optimisation layer, not the foundation.

4. Free 7-day provisioning: needs hands-on Xcode on a Mac and weekly re-signing; not viable here.

Language/API: Rust + wgpu + WGSL is the only surveyed stack where one codebase and one shader language target both Safari WebGPU and native Metal with first-class support (wgpu v30.0.2, 12 Nov 2026). C++ with Emscripten/emdawnwebgpu has stable WASM threads but a weaker native-iOS story; Slang's Metal and WGSL targets are still marked experimental.

Determinism: CPU float determinism across ARM64, x86-64 and WASM is achievable and is what shipped engines do (no FMA contraction, no fast-math, own trig, fixed ordering; Jolt reports about 8% cost). GPU float results cannot be bit-exact across devices under the WGSL spec, so authoritative state must be CPU-side or integer on GPU.

Research limits: the shared web-search budget ran out late in the session, and only GitHub-hosted sources could be opened directly. webkit.org and caniuse.com have to be allowed in the session's network settings for direct checks, and developer.apple.com was not attempted after those failed, so Apple-documentation claims rest on search-result excerpts and are marked verified=false. No public Safari-WebGPU-versus-native-Metal compute benchmark was found, and the iPhone 15's actual adapter limits were not measured.

### Implications for the design
- Structure the engine as a platform-agnostic core library plus thin hosts: a web host (wasm32 + browser WebGPU) for the first demo and a native iOS host (Metal via wgpu) for the long-term path. Rust + wgpu + WGSL gives both from one codebase; anything host-specific (haptics, audio, threads, storage) goes behind a platform interface.
- Make the first deliverable a one-page capability probe on GitHub Pages that prints adapter.limits, features, crossOriginIsolated, hardwareConcurrency and a memory-growth test on the user's actual iPhone 15. Every budget below is inferred from other A16 devices or WebKit source, not measured on that phone.
- Size GPU data structures for the web floor: any single buffer or binding at most 256 MiB guaranteed (1 GiB likely on A16), workgroups of at most 1024 invocations with 32 KB shared storage. Always request requiredLimits explicitly from adapter.limits; otherwise the spec defaults (128 MiB storage binding) apply.
- Budget the web build at roughly 1 GB total process memory (WASM heap plus GPU buffers share the 6 GB unified RAM and the process is killed near 2 GB). Declare a modest WebAssembly.Memory maximum, never 4 GB, and do not plan on Memory64 or relaxed SIMD until Safari 27.3 ships.
- Treat a tab kill as the normal failure mode, not device.lost: checkpoint world state to OPFS/IndexedDB, call navigator.storage.persist(), and make resume-from-snapshot a core engine feature (it is also what deterministic replay needs).
- Run physics on a fixed timestep decoupled from rendering and make 30 fps presentation a supported mode, because Low Power Mode and thermal mitigation halve requestAnimationFrame on iOS.
- Plan the web thread pool for at most 4 workers and gate it on crossOriginIsolated with a single-thread fallback. Starting single-threaded avoids both the coi-serviceworker reload dance and the Rust-nightly requirement for wasm threads; cross-origin isolation later also buys 20 µs timers for profiling.
- Write WGSL to WebKit's stricter dialect from day one (no textures or samplers in let bindings, constant arrays of 2,047 elements or fewer, large tables in buffers) and test shaders on real Safari early; passing naga or Chrome validation does not mean Safari will compile them.
- Split state by determinism class: rigid-body, constraint and gameplay-authoritative state on the CPU with strict IEEE rules (no FMA contraction, no fast-math, in-house sin/cos/atan2, fixed iteration and insertion order), and bulk continuum work (particles, grids, fields) on the GPU with tolerance-based, non-bit-exact guarantees or integer/fixed-point where exactness matters.
- Use the WASM build as the cross-platform determinism reference: its float semantics are fixed by spec, so native ARM64 and x86-64 builds can be regression-tested against WASM state hashes in the Linux container.
- For the native track, budget for the Apple Developer Program (US$99/yr) and a public repo (free macOS runners) or about US$0.062 per macOS minute; the pipeline is git push, macOS runner build and sign with an App Store Connect Team API key, TestFlight, install on the phone, with a fresh build at least every 90 days.
- Keep an escape hatch below wgpu on native (raw Metal 3/4 kernels, larger buffers, Core Haptics, low-latency audio, all 6 cores with NEON). Metal 4 is optional on A16 and mainly reduces CPU-side overhead, so it is an optimisation, not a requirement.
- Design game feel without haptics for the web build (navigator.vibrate and gamepad haptics are absent on iPhone) and expose haptics only through the native host.
- Avoid Slang as the shader source of truth for now (Metal and WGSL targets experimental) and avoid a Swift-only or Metal-only core, which cannot be built or tested in the Linux container.

## Rigid bodies, articulated mechanisms, contact and friction (solver families, sim-to-real trust, mobile ARM performance, determinism, physical sub-models, actuators, formats, coupling)

Recommendation: for mechanisms on an iPhone 15, the best realism per millisecond is a CPU, substepped soft-constraint Gauss-Seidel solver (the TGS-soft / "Soft Step" lineage of Box2D v3, Box3D and Rapier: semi-implicit substeps, warm starting, soft constraints, relax pass, graph-coloured NEON SIMD), extended with reduced-coordinate (Featherstone) articulations and physical actuator models for robots. That combination is what robotics stacks rely on (PhysX TGS + articulations, MuJoCo, Newton). It is the only family with published Apple-silicon throughput (Box3D on an M2: about 555 stacked boxes, 170 piled convex hulls or 900 jointed bodies per ms per core at 4 substeps), default cross-platform bitwise determinism, and proven stacking. XPBD-rigid has the cleanest physical compliance, but its authors and Erin Catto report stack, friction and float-precision problems. AVBD is promising for a GPU-unified rigid+soft solver but is experimental, with no mobile numbers. IPC/ABD give guarantees at roughly 35x to over 10,000x beyond the real-time budget on desktop CPUs. MuJoCo/Drake-style convex soft contact is the fidelity reference for articulated robots, not for thousands of free bodies. No engine is first-principles at the contact: all regularise stiffness by 4-6 orders of magnitude and treat Coulomb friction and restitution as empirical sub-models, and fitted simulators still miss real cube tosses by 13-25% of object size. The engine should therefore derive parameters from SI material data, clamp to what the substep rate resolves, and report the clamp. Couple to the deformable/fluid substrate in two tiers: constraint-level coupling inside the same substep loop for attached deformables, and partitioned momentum exchange (collider SDF/velocity out, accumulated wrenches in, asynchronous rates allowed) for bulk MPM/particle media on the GPU. Verification caveat: only GitHub-hosted sources could be fetched directly this session (box2d.org, arxiv.org, graphics.cs.utah.edu, newton-physics.github.io and matthias-research.github.io were blocked by the network allowlist, and the shared web-search budget ran out). Claims from sources I could not open are marked verified=false; URDF/SDFormat limits, hydraulic actuator models and the AVBD paper's tables remain unchecked.

- XPBD (2016) ties constraint compliance to an elastic potential so stiffness is independent of time step and iteration count and yields constraint-force estimates. Small Steps (2019) showed n substeps x 1 iteration beats 1 step x n iterations: for a 1:1000 mass-ratio rigid chain, 100 iterations took 4 ms and visibly separated, 500 iterations took 17 ms and still stretched, 100 substeps x 1 iteration took 6 ms and was stiff; stated limits are that velocity error shrinks only linearly with dt and the dt^2 term can exhaust float32. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matthias-research/pages/HEAD/publications/XPBD.pdf + https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matthias-research/pages/HEAD/publications/smallsteps.pdf
- "Detailed Rigid Body Simulation with XPBD" (Mueller et al. 2020) uses 20 substeps x 1 iteration per 1/60 s frame (40 for pendula): 0.4 ms/frame for a robot arm, 3.5 ms for a rope, 18 ms for an RC car with soft tyres on a 3.6 GHz Core i7, and it drives a steering linkage with a 1:760 mass ratio. Its own stated drawbacks: reduced numerical damping leaves high-frequency jitter, small steps require double precision, and per-projection constraint updates "might cause instabilities when simulating tall stacks or piles of objects". — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matthias-research/pages/HEAD/publications/PBDBodies.pdf
- Erin Catto's Solver2D (Feb 2024) compared 8 solvers at 60 Hz (PGS, PGS_NGS, PGS_NGS_Block, PGS_Soft, TGS_Sticky, TGS_Soft, TGS_NGS, XPBD) and chose TGS_Soft (substepping + warm starting + soft constraints + relaxation, later renamed Soft Step) for Box2D v3, reporting trouble getting XPBD friction to work and floating artefacts from deriving velocity from positions. Box2D 3.0 notes say Soft Step is more stable than v2.4 for mass ratios, chains and stacks and over 2x faster, but slightly behind the old NGS block solver for vertical stacks. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erincatto/erincatto.github.io/master/posts/2024/02/solver2d/index.html + .../posts/2024/08/releasing-box2d-3.0/index.html
- Box3D (MIT, C17) was announced in late June 2026 and is at v0.1.0, which the author calls alpha: Soft Step solver, convex hulls / triangle meshes / height fields, graph colouring, wide SIMD (SSE2/AVX2/NEON), double-precision positions, recording/replay and cross-platform determinism. Joints are limited to revolute, prismatic, distance, motor, weld and wheel with an improved joint solver still planned, and the README lists only 64-bit Windows/Linux/macOS plus an Emscripten build (no iOS target named). — [high] https://github.com/erincatto/box3d + https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erincatto/erincatto.github.io/master/posts/2026/06/announcing-box3d/index.html
- AVBD (Giles, Diaz, Yuksel; ACM TOG 44(4), Aug 2025) adds an augmented-Lagrangian dual update to Vertex Block Descent so hard contacts/joints and high stiffness ratios converge in a few implicit-Euler iterations; reported results are 110,000 blocks at 3.5 ms solve (9.8 ms with collision detection) and 510,000 blocks at 10.3 ms, with 4 iterations per frame on an RTX 4090. The paper notes hard constraints can inject energy through position correction (limited by a stabilisation parameter alpha) and that the hard-constraint Hessian needs an approximation to stay positive definite. — [medium, unverified] https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf (host blocked; search excerpts only)
- As of Nov 2026 every AVBD implementation I could open is explicitly non-production: the authors' 2D/3D demos are "not intended to be a super optimized implementation", Newton's SolverVBD (AVBD rigid) is marked experimental with limited joint support, three-avbd (WebGPU) lists no CCD, no sleeping and no cross-device bitwise determinism, and webphysics is a Chrome-only proof of concept. None publishes CPU or mobile-GPU timings. — [high] https://github.com/savant117/avbd-demo3d ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/newton-physics/newton/main/docs/solvers/index.rst ; https://github.com/sbobyn/three-avbd ; https://github.com/jure/webphysics
- MuJoCo uses generalized coordinates, where joint constraints "cannot be violated", and a convex soft-contact model that drops strict complementarity; defaults are a 2 ms timestep and solref 0.02 s / damping ratio 1, with the constraint time constant required to be at least 2x the timestep. Its docs concede that normal force and velocity can both be positive and "the only way to initiate slip is to generate some motion in the normal direction", and recommend elliptic cones, high impratio and the Newton solver (usually about 5 iterations, rarely over 20) to suppress slip. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/doc/computation/index.rst + doc/modeling.rst + doc/overview.rst
- MuJoCo is at 4.0.1 (11 Nov 2026); 2026 releases added a `discrete` integrator merging the constraint solve with the velocity update (3.13.0), an experimental penetration-free `ipc` flex-contact mode (3.14.0), sleeping-island work, and a `dcmotor` actuator with back-EMF, current saturation, cogging, inductance and thermal effects (3.12.0-4.0.0). Guidance: about 2 ms steps for contact-rich manipulation (0.5-2 ms for stiff grippers), 5-15 ms for locomotion, implicitfast by default, RK4 when long-horizon energy conservation of tumbling bodies matters. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/doc/changelog.rst + doc/modeling.rst + doc/computation/index.rst

- Affine Body Dynamics (Lan et al. 2022) reports 124x to over 1,200x speed-ups over rigid-IPC on CPU and per-frame times near Bullet's in one test (about 2.2 ms vs 1.5 ms at 1/240 s); its GPU successors are CUDA-bound (StiffGIPC: up to 10x over prior GPU IPC; libuipc). Offset Geometric Contact (2025) reaches 6.3 ms per step for a 246K-vertex cloth stack on an RTX 4090 (0.21 s on CPU), about two orders of magnitude faster than IPC, but only while per-step motion stays within the contact radius. — [medium, unverified] https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10022v1 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06224 ; https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/ogc/ (search excerpts only; hosts blocked)
- Genesis (academic release Dec 2024; Apache-2.0; rigid, FEM, MPM, PBD/SPH, uipc and a SAP coupler in one Python scene, compiled to CUDA/Metal/Vulkan/x86/ARM64) advertised 43M FPS, which an independent analysis reduced to about 0.29M FPS (150x lower) under representative settings and measured as 3-10x slower than ManiSkill on cube picking. Its IPC coupling requires an NVIDIA GPU on Linux/Windows x86 per the README. — [medium, unverified] https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/Genesis (opened) ; https://stoneztao.substack.com/p/the-new-hyped-genesis-simulator-is (search excerpt only)
- Engines bolt extra empirical terms onto Coulomb friction: MuJoCo's condim 4/6 add torsional and rolling friction whose coefficients have units of length (contact-patch radii; rolling default 0.0001), Box2D v3.1 caps rolling-resistance torque at coefficient x radius x normal force, and XPBD-rigid applies static friction positionally while lambda_t < mu_s * lambda_n with dynamic friction in a separate velocity pass. Drake's convex discrete solvers drop the static coefficient entirely. — [high] MuJoCo doc/modeling.rst ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erincatto/box2d/main/docs/simulation.md ; PBDBodies.pdf ; Drake contact_model_doxygen.h (all opened)
- Restitution is approximated everywhere: Box2D/Box3D solve it after the main pass from pre-solve approach speed (approximate with multiple contacts, zeroed below a threshold), and XPBD-rigid sets e = 0 when |v_n| <= 2|g|h. Resolvable stiffness is tied to step rate in every engine read: Box2D/Box3D and Jolt cap soft-constraint frequency at half the step frequency, MuJoCo's default contact time constant is 20 ms (10x its 2 ms step), and Drake warns fixed-step penalty contact becomes unstable above about one tenth of the contact time scale. — [high] Box2D and Box3D docs/simulation.md ; PBDBodies.pdf ; Jolt Architecture.md ; MuJoCo doc/modeling.rst ; Drake contact_model_doxygen.h (all opened)
- Coulomb friction is an empirical law that omits the Stribeck effect, drilling friction and viscous terms, and rigid bodies with Coulomb friction can have no solution or several (Painleve paradox), resolved only by impulsive forces or contact compliance. The coefficient of restitution is not a material constant: for viscoelastic bodies it tends to 1 as impact speed goes to 0, and in the plastic regime it falls roughly as v^(-1/4). — [medium, unverified] https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.03545 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0204105 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0701142 (search excerpts only; host blocked)
- Box2D 3.0 replaced its CCD with a hybrid speculative + time-of-impact scheme that "seems to prevent almost all dynamic versus static tunneling"; dynamic-vs-dynamic CCD is off by default and opt-in bullets are less robust (same policy in Box3D). Jolt's LinearCast uses the starting orientation so fast-rotating long objects can still tunnel, and PhysX CCD is disabled when GPU dynamics is on. — [high] erincatto.github.io posts/2024/08/releasing-box2d-3.0 ; Box3D docs/simulation.md ; Jolt Architecture.md ; IsaacLab docs/source/concepts/solver_differences.rst (all opened via GitHub)
- Shape choice dominates cost and stability: in Box3D's M2 data a pile of 5,120 convex hulls costs about 5.9 microseconds per body-step versus 1.8 for stacked boxes (3.3x, my division); Jolt calls dynamic meshes fairly expensive and cylinders its least stable shape; Drake's hydroelastic cost scales with the number of contact-surface faces. SDF contact for non-convex parts appears only as experimental or plugin paths in the sources opened (three-avbd's SDF playground is capped at 36 bodies; MuJoCo ships an SDF plugin). — [high] Box3D benchmark CSVs ; Jolt Architecture.md ; Drake contact_model_doxygen.h ; https://github.com/sbobyn/three-avbd ; MuJoCo changelog.rst
- MuJoCo's actuator model separates transmission, activation dynamics and force generation, and its tuning guide states that geared actuators add reflected rotor inertia I_rotor * N^2 that "often exceeds the link inertia of wrists, fingers, and ankles", so realistic armature "improves realism, mass-matrix conditioning, and timestep stability for free". Viscous joint damping is integrated implicitly at no solver cost, whereas dry frictionloss adds a constraint row per joint. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/doc/modeling.rst + doc/overview.rst
- MJCF is being ingested beyond MuJoCo (Rapier 0.35.3 handles MJCF frictionloss as a constraint; Newton ships a MuJoCo-to-USD converter), while MuJoCo's OpenUSD support is still labelled experimental and uses standard UsdPhysics plus mjcPhysics extensions, with Newton's USD schemas acting as staging for solver-agnostic additions (REP-158 discussion, June 2026). Only the Rapier changelog entry and MuJoCo's USD decoder plugin were opened directly; the rest is from search excerpts. — [medium, unverified] https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/latest/OpenUSD/mjcPhysics.html ; https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/discussions/3360 ; https://github.com/newton-physics/mujoco-usd-converter (search excerpts) ; Rapier CHANGELOG.md and MuJoCo changelog.rst (opened)
- Published rigid-deformable coupling in the substep solver family is done at constraint level inside the substep loop: Small Steps notes that liquid and rigid constraint lists are "simply concatenated or interleaved, allowing fine-grained coupling", Mueller 2020 couples soft tyres to rigid rims this way, Rapier 0.36 simulates soft bodies together with rigid bodies, contacts and joints, and Newton's VBD handles cloth, soft and rigid bodies in one solver. Jolt's XPBD soft bodies collide with rigid bodies only (no soft-soft collision, no buoyancy), and Isaac Lab lists Newton's MPM with rigid bodies as colliders only. — [high] smallsteps.pdf ; PBDBodies.pdf ; Rapier CHANGELOG.md ; newton docs/solvers/index.rst ; Jolt Architecture.md ; IsaacLab solver_differences.rst (all opened)

### Implications for the design
- Core solver: build on a CPU, substepped soft-constraint Gauss-Seidel solver (TGS-soft / Soft Step lineage: semi-implicit Euler per substep, warm starting, soft constraints, relax pass, graph-coloured 4-wide NEON), 60 Hz frames with 4-8 substeps globally and 16-32 for flagged precision islands. Keep XPBD-rigid as a reference for position-level compliance, not as the primary contact solver.
- Budget: the M2 data gives about 1,030 boxes/ms or 325 convex hulls/ms on two performance cores at 4 substeps. Plan the iPhone 15 rigid slice at 3-4 ms, i.e. roughly 3,000 awake boxes or 1,000 hulls, fewer at 8 substeps (solver cost scales with substeps, collision does not), before thermal derating and any WebAssembly overhead. Islands, sleeping and per-island substep counts are mandatory.
- Mechanism path: add reduced-coordinate articulations (Featherstone ABA/CRBA) as first-class bodies inside the same substep loop, with exact joints, armature = N^2 * rotor inertia, implicit joint damping, and friction-loss rows only where stiction matters. Apply contacts and loop closures as soft constraints through the articulation's impulse response; keep maximal-coordinate joints for breakable, loosely constrained or loop-heavy assemblies.
- Actuators belong to the physics layer: DC motor (voltage, back-EMF, winding resistance, current limit giving the linear torque-speed envelope), gearbox (ratio, reflected inertia, efficiency, backlash as a soft equality), springs/dampers and fluid-power cylinders as stateful models, following MuJoCo's transmission + activation + force-generation split. Robotics evidence says actuator fidelity dominates sim-to-real error.
- Materials-first parameterisation: author density, Young's modulus, Poisson ratio, friction pairs and dissipation in SI, derive contact stiffness/damping (Hertz + Hunt-Crossley) and joint compliance from them, then clamp to what the substep rate resolves (soft-constraint frequency at most 1/2 to 1/4 of substep rate) and report the clamp. Steel-on-steel stiffness cannot be resolved at 480-960 Hz; effective contact moduli will be in the 1e5-1e7 Pa range that Drake also accepts.
- Label friction and restitution as empirical sub-models: Coulomb cone with persistent anchors, optional static/kinetic and Stribeck curve, torsional and rolling resistance scaled by the computed contact-patch radius, restitution from contact dissipation with a resting-velocity threshold. Keep the normal direction compliant rather than perfectly rigid to avoid Painleve-type non-existence.
- Energy policy: no hidden numerical damping in the rigid core (semi-implicit substeps, implicit gyroscopic term), all dissipation explicit and material-derived. Ship a conformance suite with energy/momentum drift budgets (triple pendulum, 1:1000 chain, heavy-on-light stack, Newton's cradle, spinning coin, card pyramid) plus a reality check against the published cube-toss data, where fitted Drake/Bullet/MuJoCo still sit at 13-25% position error.
- Collision: analytic primitives and convex hulls first (hull piles cost about 3.3x boxes per body), convex decomposition for concave dynamic parts, triangle meshes and height fields static-only, SDF contact reserved for gears/threads after a prototype. Use hybrid speculative + time-of-impact CCD against statics with opt-in bullets, and run broad/narrow phase once per frame with speculative margins.
- Determinism: keep the rigid/mechanism core on CPU in float32 with strict floating-point rules (no fast-math, FMA contraction off, own sin/cos/atan2, creation-order processing). Because development happens on x64 Linux without a Mac, x64-to-ARM64 bit-exactness is what lets CI golden trajectories stand in for on-device behaviour; treat the GPU substrate as non-deterministic and isolate it behind the coupling interface.
- Coupling tier A (attached deformables: tyres, belts, cables, pads, cloth on mechanisms): solve them in the same substep loop as compliant particle/FEM constraints interleaved with the rigid sweep, sharing the SI material model, as Mueller 2020, Rapier 0.36 and Jolt do.
- Coupling tier B (bulk continuum: MPM/SPH/PBF fluid, granular, large soft volumes, likely on GPU): partitioned momentum exchange. Rigid bodies export collider SDF + velocity, the substrate returns per-body wrenches accumulated over its own steps, applied as impulses at rigid substep boundaries, with asynchronous rates allowed and explicit momentum bookkeeping.
- Do not base the phone runtime on IPC/ABD, Newton, Genesis or libuipc: IPC costs 1.4-776 s per step on desktop CPUs and the fast variants are CUDA/Python-bound. Use them as offline ground truth and for asset compatibility (MJCF import first, USD Physics second).
- Treat AVBD as a time-boxed R&D track (Metal/WebGPU unified rigid + soft), not the baseline: no mobile numbers, implicit-Euler damping, experimental joints and no bitwise determinism as of Nov 2026.
- Build-vs-adopt input: Box3D (MIT, C17, alpha v0.1, Soft Step, NEON, deterministic, Emscripten build, few joint types), Jolt (iOS ARM64 supported, deterministic including WASM, mature joints/vehicles, sequential impulse) and Rapier (Rust, multibodies + soft bodies + determinism) are viable bases or reference implementations. None provides material-derived contact or actuator physics, which is the layer this engine must add.

### Open questions
- Real iPhone 15 throughput: sustained (thermally throttled) bodies per ms for a Soft-Step-style solver on 2 performance + 4 efficiency cores, native versus WebAssembly (128-bit SIMD, threads). No published A16 or iPhone physics benchmark was found; the M2 figures are a proxy.
- Substep count versus fidelity for mechanisms: how many substeps do gear trains, 1:1000 mass-ratio linkages and stiff springs need, and does float32 hold? Macklin and Mueller both warn about precision at small steps, and NEON doubles halve SIMD width.
- AVBD on the A16 GPU: iterations and ms for 1k-10k bodies, energy dissipation versus substepped TGS-soft, joint and motor fidelity. The AVBD paper could not be opened (host blocked) and I found no independent AVBD-versus-Soft-Step comparison.
- Articulation + contact coupling cost: per-substep cost of impulse-response contacts for 20-60 DoF robots, and whether loop closures as soft constraints are accurate enough or need a Kamino-style Proximal-ADMM or direct solve.
- Can material-derived Hertz/Hunt-Crossley contact be resolved at 480-960 Hz for the intended material range, or is a hybrid needed (compliant where resolvable, impulse-level with velocity-dependent restitution tables elsewhere)? What stiffness clamp is mechanically acceptable?
- Friction-model stability: do static/kinetic + Stribeck and patch-scaled torsional/rolling friction stay stable in a substepped Gauss-Seidel solver with 2-4 points per manifold, and how large is the error against real data (cube toss, inclined plane, grasp tests)?
- Rigid-substrate coupling: stability of partitioned impulse exchange for light bodies in dense fluid, momentum conservation at mismatched rates, and whether attached deformables must live on the CPU with the rigid core for determinism.
- Non-convex contact for meshing gears and threads on mobile (SDF or otherwise): cost and robustness are unknown; the only SDF path opened is experimental and capped at tens of bodies.
- Determinism scope: do games on this engine need replay or rollback? Box2D/Box3D offer cross-platform but not rollback determinism, and a GPU substrate breaks bitwise guarantees.
- Delivery constraint: if the runtime ships as a PWA, are WASM threads (cross-origin isolation headers, which GitHub Pages does not let you set directly; from memory, unverified) and WebGPU usable in Safari on the user's iPhone 15? This decides whether the two-core multithreaded numbers are reachable.

## First-principles rigor, verification & validation, and game-substrate design for a phone-scale "reality engine"

Research conditions: the shared WebSearch budget was already exhausted (0 searches ran) and the session network blocked most sites, so I fetched known primary URLs directly. With the user's approval on the permission card, four hosts were added to the stored "pulsevault" environment allowlist (en.wikipedia.org, arxiv.org, homes.cs.washington.edu, www.gdcvault.com) - a persistent change. Still blocked: roboti.us (Erez 2015 PDF), doi.org and publisher sites (Chung & Pollard, Martin & Moyce, Lube, Lajeunesse primaries), press coverage of the TotK GDC talk. Those items are marked verified=false or cited via secondary sources.

Defensible definition of "first-principles" for this engine: (1) the only hard-coded physics are the continuum balance laws (mass, linear momentum, angular momentum, energy) plus the second law as an admissibility test on every model; (2) everything object-specific enters only through geometry and constitutive models whose parameters are measured SI material properties with provenance - never per-object scripted behaviour; (3) every discretisation converges to its governing equation at a tested order, and every non-convergent shortcut (capped sound speed, constraint softening, sleeping) is declared with an error budget; (4) every fidelity claim is backed by an automated test against a closed-form or experimental reference with a tolerance; (5) dimensional consistency is enforced by types. It explicitly does not mean ab-initio/atomistic simulation.

Fidelity ladder. Tier 0, exact to round-off by construction: mass, linear/angular momentum exchange between interacting pairs, units, same-build determinism. Tier 1, convergent and verified against closed forms: free rigid-body motion, joints/mechanisms, small-strain elasticity (deflection, buckling, modes), heat conduction, hydrostatics/buoyancy, laminar viscous flow. Tier 2, empirical closures with measured parameters, validated against experiments: Coulomb friction, restitution/contact dissipation, plasticity/fracture thresholds, granular rheology, viscosity, phase change, lumped circuits/motors, modal sound damping. Tier 3, declared phenomenological sub-grid models: turbulence, aerodynamic lift/drag on bodies below fluid resolution, one-step combustion, sub-grid surface tension, capped stiffness for metals/water. Out of scope: quantum/atomistic, relativistic, Maxwell field solves, shocks/compressible flow, resolved turbulence (DNS), detailed chemistry, wave optics.

### Findings
- Continuum mechanics is governed by balance laws for mass, momentum and energy; kinematic relations and constitutive equations are needed to close the system, and the second law (Clausius-Duhem inequality) restricts which constitutive relations are thermodynamically allowable. So only the balance laws are 'fundamental' at this scale - every material response is a closure. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_mechanics ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausius%E2%80%93Duhem_inequality
- Coulomb friction is 'an approximate model' with mu 'an empirical property of the contacting materials', restitution is 'a property of a pair of objects, not a single object', and rigid bodies + Coulomb friction are mutually inconsistent in some configurations (Painleve paradox). MuJoCo's docs say the Coulomb model 'is not well-defined in continuous time', frictional LCP is NP-hard, and LCP and convex contact models are both 'different approximations to physical reality'. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friction ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_restitution ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/doc/computation/index.rst
- Where a phone must cut: all-atom molecular dynamics uses ~1 fs steps (about 1.7e13 steps per 60 Hz frame); continuum flow holds for Knudsen number < 0.01; resolved turbulence (DNS) needs N^3 >= Re^(9/4) grid points with operations growing as Re^3, so turbulence must be a closure model. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_drift ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knudsen_number ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_numerical_simulation
- Explicit schemes need Courant number <= ~1; with measured sound speeds of ~5,930 m/s (steel) and 1,481 m/s (water), a 1 cm resolution forces dt <= 1.7 microseconds (~10,000 substeps per 60 Hz frame) for real steel and 6.8 microseconds (~2,500) for real water (my arithmetic from the cited values). Standard practice in weakly compressible SPH is to lower the numerical sound speed so Mach < 0.1, allowing <1% density variation. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant%E2%80%93Friedrichs%E2%80%93Lewy_condition ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoothed-particle_hydrodynamics
- Metal Shading Language 4.1 (spec dated 2026-06-04): 'Metal does not support the double, long long, unsigned long long, and long double data types'. Fast math is the default (assumes no NaN/INF, allows reassociation and FMA contraction), denormals may be flushed to zero, and math functions are only accurate to ~4-6 ULP; -fno-fast-math / -fmetal-math-mode=safe disables the unsafe optimisations. — [high] https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Shading-Language-Specification.pdf
- float32 has a 24-bit significand; Godot's table gives a position step of ~0.5 mm at 4-8 km and ~2 mm at 16-32 km from origin. Jolt says float is 'accurate enough' within ~5 km and its double-position mode costs 5-10%; Box2D keeps all heavy math in relative float32 coordinates with double world positions for 'a few percent', and Jolt's cross-platform determinism option costs ~8%. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot-docs/master/tutorials/physics/large_world_coordinates.rst ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Docs/Architecture.md ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erincatto/box2d/main/docs/large_worlds.md
- Semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler is only first order but 'almost conserves the energy'; for a harmonic oscillator it exactly preserves a modified energy and shifts frequency by a factor 1 + (omega*dt)^2/24. Symplectic integrators conserve a slightly perturbed 'shadow' Hamiltonian (bounded error, no secular drift), whereas non-symplectic families such as Runge-Kutta show substantial drift; variational integrators are symplectic and momentum-preserving. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-implicit_Euler_method ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_drift ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_integrator
- Macklin et al. (SCA 2019): 'first order implicit methods introduce numerical damping', and n substeps with one XPBD iteration each give significantly less constraint error and damping than one step with n iterations. Muller et al. 2020 show pendula with 40 substeps x 1 iteration 'keep on swinging for a long time' while 1 substep x 100 iterations 'come to rest very quickly'. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matthias-research/pages/master/publications/smallsteps.pdf ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matthias-research/pages/master/publications/PBDBodies.pdf
- MuJoCo docs: semi-implicit Euler is 'strictly better' than explicit Euler; RK4 is 'qualitatively better' for energy-conserving systems even at equal compute, while the implicit integrator 'loses energy'. Their implicit free-body treatment damps tumbling motion at a rate ~(h*|omega|)^2 per step, so long-horizon tumbling (the Dzhanibekov case) needs RK4. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/doc/computation/index.rst
- NIST's FDS Verification Guide (using ASTM E1355 definitions): 'verification is a check of the math; validation is a check of the physics', and its verification cases run nightly in CI with pass/fail against exact values within a tolerance. ASME V&V 10-2006 covers computational solid mechanics and V&V 20-2009 covers CFD/heat transfer; the Method of Manufactured Solutions (Roache 2002) adds source terms so a chosen analytic field is the exact solution, letting a test measure the observed order of convergence. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firemodels/fds/master/Manuals/FDS_Verification_Guide/FDS_Verification_Guide.tex ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verification_and_validation_of_computer_simulation_models ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/master/doc/source/examples/incompressible-flow/2d-mms/2d-mms.rst
- Le Lidec et al. (arXiv 2304.06372, v3 July 2024): rigid contact is defined by the Signorini condition, Coulomb's law and the maximum dissipation principle, and every engine relaxes one of them. A pyramidal (LCP) cone biases friction direction; the convex CCP relaxation produces unphysical normal forces and bouncing on a sliding cube with a 'significant gap' from the analytic energy curve; only the NCP formulation solved with PGS matched the analytic result. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06372
- Acosta, Yang & Posa (RA-L/IROS 2022) validated Drake, MuJoCo and Bullet against an open dataset of 550 tosses of a 10 cm acrylic cube: simulators capture inelastic impacts 'surprisingly well, though generally fail to reproduce elasticity'. Fazeli et al. 2017 found six common rigid contact models have a task-specific accuracy ceiling and parameters that are 'difficult to give a physical interpretation'. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00541 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04979
- Kaup et al. (arXiv 2407.08590, 2024) review nine engines (Brax, Chrono, Gazebo, MuJoCo, ODE, PhysX, PyBullet, Webots, Unity), rating MuJoCo the leader and Unity easy but lacking simulation fidelity. They summarise Erez/Tassa/Todorov 2015 as comparing speed, stability and accuracy of Bullet, MuJoCo, ODE and PhysX on identical scenarios by the timestep at which errors appear, with MuJoCo best especially for many-joint systems (primary PDF host roboti.us was blocked). — [medium] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08590
- Chung & Pollard, 'Predictable behavior during contact simulation: a comparison of selected physics engines' (Computer Animation and Virtual Worlds, 2016) compared Bullet, DART, MuJoCo and ODE on simple contact scenes and found no engine uniformly best, with behaviour differing visibly even in simple cases and across timesteps. — [medium, unverified] memory (https://doi.org/10.1002/cav.1712 - host blocked)
- Rigid-body closed forms for the test suite: pendulum T = 2*pi*sqrt(L/g)*(1 + theta0^2/16 + 11*theta0^4/3072 + ...); drop-bounce e = sqrt(h/H); incline slip threshold tan(theta) = mu_s; intermediate-axis (Dzhanibekov) instability with angular-momentum magnitude and kinetic energy both conserved. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendulum_(mechanics) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_restitution ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_repose ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_racket_theorem
- Structural closed forms: cantilever tip deflection P*L^3/(3EI) (uniform load q*L^4/(8EI)); Euler buckling P_cr = pi^2*EI/(KL)^2; cantilever first mode beta1*L = 0.596864*pi, omega1 = 3.5160/L^2 * sqrt(EI/mu); Hertz sphere contact F = (4/3)*E'*sqrt(R)*d^(3/2) with contact radius a^3 = 3FR/(4E'). — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler%E2%80%93Bernoulli_beam_theory ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler%27s_critical_load ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_mechanics
- Fluid closed forms: Archimedes buoyancy = weight of displaced fluid; Torricelli efflux v = sqrt(2gh); Poiseuille dp = 8*mu*L*Q/(pi*R^4) (valid when L/R > Re/48); Stokes terminal velocity v = (2/9)*(rho_p - rho_f)*g*R^2/mu at very low Re. The 2D Taylor-Green vortex is an exact Navier-Stokes solution decaying as exp(-2*nu*k^2*t) (kinetic energy as its square); the 3D case at Re = 1600 is compared against spectral reference data (Wang et al. 2013). — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen%E2%80%93Poiseuille_equation ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stokes%27_law ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor%E2%80%93Green_vortex ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/master/doc/source/examples/incompressible-flow/3d-taylor-green-vortex/3d-taylor-green-vortex.rst
- Experimental free-surface and granular references: the Martin & Moyce 1952 dam break (dimensionless front position vs time) is a standard automated comparison. Granular column run-out depends only on aspect ratio a: quasi-2D (L_f-L_0)/L_0 ~ 1.2a for a < ~2.3 and ~1.9*a^(2/3) above; axisymmetric ~1.24a (a < 1.7) then ~1.6*a^(1/2) (Lube) or ~1.35a (a < 0.74) then ~2.0*a^(1/2) (Lajeunesse); prefactors vary with material but exponents are robust. Caveat: the secondary source prints the axisymmetric tall-column exponent as '1.2'; 1/2 is from memory of the primary papers. — [medium] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kks32/phd-thesis/master/Chapter2/chapter2.tex ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/master/doc/source/examples/multiphysics/dam-break/dam-break.rst
- Ready-made particle/contact verification cases exist: MFIX DEM02 (bouncing particle vs analytic hard-sphere bounce heights), DEM05 (oblique impact vs Kharaz et al. 2001 experiments), ten Cate et al. 2002 (1.5 cm nylon sphere settling in a 10x16x10 cm tank, PIV data), Zuriguel sandpile angle of repose, Evangelista granular dam break. Project Chrono keeps a separate validation repo with MSC ADAMS reference trajectories for each joint type. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/master/doc/source/examples/dem/bouncing-particle/bouncing-particle.rst ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/master/doc/source/examples/sharp-immersed-boundary/sedimentation-1-particle/sedimentation-1-particle.rst ; https://github.com/projectchrono/chrono-validation
- Heat is cheap: explicit conduction (FTCS) is stable iff dt <= h^2/(2*alpha) in 1D and h^2/(6*alpha) in 3D; with diffusivities of 111 (copper), 18.8 (steel), 19 (air) and 0.143 mm^2/s (water), a 1 cm grid allows dt up to 0.15 s even for copper, i.e. stable at 60 Hz, while a 1 mm copper grid needs <= 1.5 ms (my arithmetic). The Stefan problem's Neumann solution, front position s(t) = 2*lambda*sqrt(alpha*t), is the closed-form melting/freezing benchmark. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTCS_scheme ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_diffusivity ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_problem

### Implications for the design
- Adopt the five-point definition in the summary as the engine's charter: balance laws hard-coded, behaviour only via geometry + constitutive models with measured SI parameters, convergent discretisations with declared shortcuts, test-backed claims, type-enforced units. Publish the fidelity ladder (Tier 0 exact, Tier 1 convergent, Tier 2 empirical closure, Tier 3 phenomenological, out of scope) in the docs and tag every feature with its tier.
- Architecture follows the physics hierarchy: a small conservation core (mass/momentum/energy bookkeeping) with pluggable constitutive modules (elasticity, plasticity, friction pair table, viscosity, thermal, granular) and a material database whose every number has a source and uncertainty. Friction and restitution must be stored per material pair, not per material.
- Real stiffness cannot be time-resolved on a phone: steel or true-compressibility water at 1 cm needs thousands of substeps per frame. Treat stiff solids as rigid/constraint or implicit bodies and liquids as incompressible or capped-sound-speed, and record this as a declared Tier 3 shortcut with a stated error (e.g. density variation < 1%).
- Time integration: fixed step with many small substeps (240-480 Hz is the shipping precedent) using a symplectic/variational update for the conservative part and implicit treatment only for dissipative terms; avoid large-step implicit or high-iteration PBD because the damping is numerical, not physical. Keep a per-step energy ledger (kinetic, potential, elastic, heat, numerical loss) so conservation is measured rather than assumed.
- Precision plan: GPU kernels are float32 only, so store state relative to a local cell/chunk origin and keep world positions in double on the CPU (the Box2D/Jolt pattern costs a few percent). Compile simulation shaders with fast-math off for NaN safety and reproducibility, and treat GPU-vs-CPU bitwise determinism as unproven until measured.
- Build the V&V suite before features, FDS-style: (a) conservation/unit tests, (b) verification against closed forms and manufactured solutions with a measured convergence order, (c) validation against experiments with tolerances (Martin & Moyce dam break, granular run-out exponent, cube-toss dataset, ten Cate settling, DEM02/DEM05). It can run headless in the Linux container on every change, with a smaller on-device subset; the output is a public fidelity report.
- Contact is the main fidelity risk: choose a formulation that keeps Signorini + Coulomb + maximum dissipation (or a compliant contact derived from material stiffness), avoid pyramidal friction cones, and gate the choice on the analytic sliding-cube energy test and the cube-toss data. Expect elastic (bouncy) impacts to be the weakest area and state that openly.
- Game-builder API should mirror how a robot meets reality: authors supply geometry, materials, joints, actuators (motors with torque limits drawing from an energy source) and sensors (force, temperature, fluid-force/centre-of-pressure style); game logic reads sensors and commands actuators. Direct state edits (teleport, set velocity) should exist only as explicitly flagged non-physical escape hatches so a 'strict' mode stays honest.
- Sim-first games repeatedly struggled to find the game (Noita: two years of directions; Teardown: every objective solvable by a straight line). Ship designer tools from the start: indestructible/boundary materials, energy and strength budgets as the natural gating mechanism, deterministic replay, and in-game physics telemetry for debugging emergent failures.
- Multi-physics priority by cost/benefit on an A16: heat conduction and phase change (stable explicitly at cm scale), lumped circuits and motors (tiny linear solves), and modal sound (offline eigen-analysis, cheap runtime resonators - the runtime cost claim is from memory) come almost free next to mechanics. Aerodynamic lift/drag on bodies and combustion should be planned as declared phenomenological models; light is a rendering budget question with no hardware ray tracing available.
- Units: SI everywhere at the API boundary with compile-time dimension types (mp-units if C++, uom if Rust; Swift Measurement is runtime-only), and nondimensionalised float32 values inside kernels so magnitudes stay near 1. Document a supported scale range the way Box2D does (objects roughly 0.1-10 m) rather than promising all scales.
- The 2025-26 state-of-the-art research simulators (Newton, MuJoCo Warp) are built on NVIDIA Warp/CUDA and are not a route to an iPhone; they are useful as cross-check references on the Linux side, not as a backbone.

### Open questions
- What body/particle counts and substep rates does a base iPhone 15 actually sustain at 60 Hz under sustained thermal load? Every tolerance in the fidelity ladder depends on this and it can only be measured on the device.
- Are float32 Metal compute kernels with fast-math off bitwise reproducible across iOS versions and against a CPU reference? Needs a prototype; the spec only guarantees correctly rounded + - * / and bounded ULP error for math functions.
- Which contact formulation gives the best physical accuracy per millisecond on the A16 - NCP with PGS, small-step XPBD, or compliant Hertz-style contact? Decide with the sliding-cube energy test and the 550-trajectory cube-toss dataset.
- What tolerance bands are reachable at phone resolution for the experimental benchmarks (dam-break front position, granular run-out exponent, settling velocity)? These numbers define what 'as close to real life as possible' can honestly claim.
- How stiff a deformable material can be simulated honestly before it must be switched to rigid-plus-modal-sound? Needs a prototype sweep over Young's modulus and resolution.
- The granular-collapse axisymmetric tall-column exponent (1/2) and Martin & Moyce's tabulated data should be confirmed from the primary papers (publisher sites and doi.org were blocked).
- TotK GDC 2024 talk specifics, Erez/Tassa/Todorov 2015 and Chung & Pollard 2016 could not be opened in primary form (roboti.us, Wiley, press sites blocked); treat those findings as memory/secondary until checked.
- Licensing and provenance of material data for a shipped app: which sources are redistributable (CoolProp and 'chemicals' are open; MatWeb is proprietary from memory; Materials Project terms unchecked)?
- Is the target 2D, 3D, or both? Most cited sandbox precedents that simulate materials exhaustively (Noita, Powder Toy, Algodoo) are 2D, and the user has not specified.
- Should numerically dissipated energy be returned to the system as heat to close the energy ledger, or only reported? This is a design decision with gameplay consequences that needs a prototype.
