# Reality engine — research on scale: from a workshop to a solar system

Research sweeps of 2026-11-24. "unverified" = from memory or a search excerpt, not checked against an opened source.

## Numerics of gravity, orbits, frames and time from tabletop to solar system and beyond (integrators, precision, non-inertial frames, time warp, planet-scale environment models, Newtonian limits, open ephemeris data, and a 0.5 ms A16 budget)

A solar system fits the phone easily; the hard parts are precision, frames and time warp, not force count. No A16 measurement of any of this exists, so every phone figure below is scaled from desktop measurements.

Defensible budget for 0.5 ms of one A16 performance core per 60 Hz frame (ESTIMATE, assuming an A16 P-core is 1-2x the single-thread speed of the desktop CPUs measured):
- **Sun + 8 planets, Wisdom-Holman at a 5-day step:** about 300-600 steps per frame, so time warp up to about 8e9x (roughly 250-500 simulated years per wall second), with relative energy error bounded near 1e-8 and no secular drift.
- **34 bodies (Sun, planets, major moons, dwarf planets), Principia-class 12th-order at a 10-minute step:** about 16 force evaluations per frame, so warp up to about 6e5x, with errors of metres per year against its own model.
- **On-rails Kepler bodies:** at least 2,500 per frame at any warp factor, exact for two bodies.
- **Direct-sum leapfrog:** about 300-450 mutually gravitating bodies at one force evaluation per frame.
- **At 1x to 1e4x warp:** the 34-body system costs under 10 microseconds per frame amortised.

Other points the architects need:
- Float32 is unusable beyond a few km (0.5 m steps at Earth radius, 16 km at 1 AU). Float64 covers a star system (31 micrometres at 1 AU, 1 mm at 30 AU) but not a galaxy (33 km at 26,000 ly). The A16 GPU is fp32-only.
- The workshop design therefore has to live in a local, co-moving, re-based frame inside a float64 hierarchical frame tree.
- Plain leapfrog is wrong for planetary orbits: about 155,000 km of along-track error per year at 100 steps per orbit.
- Newtonian scope can be declared with numbers: Mercury 42.98 arcsec per century, 499 s light-time per AU, speeds below 0.01c.

### Findings
- MEASURED: REBOUND WHFast integrates the Sun + 8 planets for 5 Gyr at a 5-day step in 6.82 days on one Xeon Gold 6148 core (WHFast512/AVX-512: 1.40 days; MERCURY: 46.5 days). DERIVED: 3.65e11 steps, so 1.61 microseconds per step (0.20 per planet), 0.33 for AVX-512 and 11 for MERCURY. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05683 (PDF opened; Table 1 and section 4)
- Principia's real-solar-system configuration integrates 34 massive bodies with a 12th-order Quinlan-Tremaine symmetric linear multistep method at a fixed 10-minute step (vessel histories: 8th order at 10 s; predictions: adaptive RKN at 1 mm and 1 mm/s tolerance). Its FAQ puts one force evaluation of all massive bodies plus one vessel at 30 microseconds (MEASURED by the authors, CPU unstated); DERIVED ceiling 600 s / 30 us = 2e7x real time on a full core, consistent with their 'very smooth at 6,000,000x with no vessels'. — [high] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mockingbirdnest/Principia/master/astronomy/sol_numerics_blueprint.proto.txt ; .../astronomy/sol_gravity_model.proto.txt ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/mockingbirdnest/Principia/Installing,-reporting-bugs,-and-frequently-asked-questions.md (all opened)
- ESTIMATE for 0.5 ms of one A16 P-core per frame (A16 taken as 1-2x the desktop cores above; unmeasured): 310-620 WHFast steps of Sun + 8 planets, i.e. warp of 8e9x to 1.6e10x at a 5-day step; or about 16 force evaluations of the 34-body system, i.e. warp of about 6e5x (range 3e5x to 1.2e6x) at a 10-minute step. Direct-sum leapfrog at an ESTIMATED 5-10 ns per pair gives 50,000-100,000 pair interactions, i.e. about 320-450 mutually gravitating bodies at one evaluation per frame. — [medium, unverified] DERIVED this session from the two measured anchors above; A16-versus-desktop ratio and ns-per-pair are estimates (Geekbench figures in 01-research.md are themselves unverified)
- Wisdom-Holman energy error is bounded and scales as O(mass-ratio x dt^2); WHFast removes the linear secular drift, leaving only a random walk from round-off (Brouwer's law). MEASURED: outer Solar System at a 1.5-day step holds about 1e-14 relative energy error with symplectic correctors and about 1e-10.5 without; Mercury needs more than 20 steps per 88-day orbit (dt about 4 days); a 5-day step kept the median relative energy error below 1e-8 over 40 Gyr. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01084 (PDF opened, sections 4.2-4.4) ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05683 (section 5)
- IAS15 is a 15th-order adaptive Gauss-Radau integrator that holds machine precision (about 1e-16) over 1e9 orbits at about 100 steps per orbit and handles close encounters and velocity-dependent forces. DERIVED cost: 7 substages x about 2 predictor-corrector iterations = about 14 force evaluations per step, about 1,400 per orbit; a 10x larger step raises the error by 1e15, so it suits a reference oracle, not a real-time integrator. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.4779 (PDF opened) ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hannorein/rebound/4.4.1/docs/integrators.md (opened)
- Close encounters are handled by hybrid schemes: MERCURIUS and TRACE run Wisdom-Holman normally and switch to IAS15 or Bulirsch-Stoer inside a critical radius (documentation example: 4 Hill radii). MEASURED: TRACE matches MERCURIUS accuracy with a 12x speed-up for planet-planet encounters and is 15x to 41x faster than IAS15 in scattering and lunar-accretion tests. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03800 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04972 ; REBOUND 4.4.1 docs/integrators.md (all opened)
- DERIVED: plain leapfrog/Verlet on a circular orbit has relative frequency error (omega*dt)^2/24 although its energy error stays bounded. For Earth's orbit that is about 155,000 km of along-track error per year at 100 steps per orbit, 1,546 km at 1,000 steps and 5.6 m at 60-second steps. Leapfrog is second-order, symplectic, time-reversible and stable only for dt < 2/omega. — [high, unverified] Derivation this session from the Verlet relation 4*sin^2(theta/2) = (omega*dt)^2; order and stability limit from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leapfrog_integration (opened)
- DERIVED position step (ULP) for float32 / float64: Earth radius 0.5 m / 0.93 nm; Moon distance 32 m / 60 nm; 1 AU 16.4 km / 31 micrometres; 30 AU 524 km / 0.98 mm; 1 light-year 1.07e9 m / 2 m; 26,000 ly 1.76e13 m (118 AU) / 32.8 km; 100,000 ly 7.0e13 m / 131 km. Velocity step: float32 is 0.49 mm/s at 7.67 km/s (LEO) and 1.95 mm/s at 29.78 km/s; float64 is 9e-13 and 3.6e-12 m/s. — [high] Direct IEEE-754 computation this session (python struct and math.ulp); float32 step sizes cross-checked against the table in https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot-docs/master/tutorials/physics/large_world_coordinates.rst (opened)

- Stock KSP uses patched conics with planets on fixed rails and no n-body gravity. To replace that, Principia had to alter the stock Jool moon system (unstable under n-body: orbits enlarged, Bop made retrograde), use a different integrator (Blanes-Moan) for that system, add double-double summation, and add geopotentials (Earth to degree 10, Moon to degree and order 30). - The IAS15 paper states that floating point destroys Galilean invariance: translating a Solar System held in AU units by 1e16 loses all relative-position information, and force accuracy can be no better than relative-position accuracy. This is the primary-source argument for storing each body relative to its parent (hierarchical frames) and never in one global frame. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.4779 (PDF opened, section 3)
- DERIVED integer ranges: int64 at 1 mm resolution spans +/-9.22e15 m (61,650 AU, 0.975 ly), enough for a star system out to the Oort cloud; int64 at 1 micrometre spans only +/-61.6 AU; int128 at 1 micrometre spans +/-1.8e16 ly. A galaxy therefore needs either 128-bit positions or a two-level address (star-system index plus an int64 or float64 offset). — [high] Integer-range arithmetic computed this session (definitional inputs)
- Surface gravity derived, not asserted: GM/R^2 = 9.820 m/s^2 at Earth's mean radius; centrifugal omega^2*R = 0.0339 m/s^2 at the equator (DERIVED); MEASURED sea-level g is 9.780 at the equator and 9.832 at the poles. The MEASURED vertical gradient is 0.3086 mGal/m (3.086e-6 per s^2), so uniform g across a 10 m workshop is wrong by about 3 ppm, while Coriolis 2*omega*v = 1.46e-3 m/s^2 at 10 m/s (1.5e-4 g) is the largest term neglected at workshop scale. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_of_Earth ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-air_gravity_anomaly (opened); derived values computed this session
- DERIVED tidal gradient at 400 km altitude: n^2 = 1.28e-6 per s^2; in the orbiting frame a vehicle is stretched radially by 3n^2 = 3.85e-6 m/s^2 per metre (0.39 micro-g per metre) and compressed by n^2 across the orbit plane. Gravity-gradient torque is 3n^2 * r x (I r), at most 1.5 n^2 * (Imax - Imin): 0.016 N*m for a 1-tonne, 10 m rod, with a libration period of orbit period / sqrt(3) = 53 min. — [high, unverified] memory (standard astrodynamics formulas); numbers computed this session from GM = 3.986e14 m^3/s^2
- Earth's J2 is 1.0826e-3 and drives node regression of -1.5*J2*(R/p)^2*n*cos(i): -3.68 degrees/day for an 800 km, 56-degree orbit (source example). DERIVED: -5.0 degrees/day for the ISS, about 590 km/day of cross-track error if J2 is omitted; the perturbing acceleration is about 1.5*J2*g = 0.016 m/s^2 at the surface. Principia found Mars's C22/S22 terms necessary for Phobos. — [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nodal_precession ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopotential_spherical_harmonic_model ; Principia Change-Log (all opened)
- A two-body orbit can be advanced exactly over any time span by a universal-variable Kepler solve (one equation covering elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic cases via Stumpff functions). WHFast512 uses a fixed 2 Halley + 2 Newton iterations with truncated series to reach 1e-13 relative error, with no library trig calls. DERIVED: at most 0.2 microseconds per body, so at least 2,500 on-rails bodies per 0.5 ms regardless of warp factor. — [medium] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_variable_formulation ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05683 Appendix A (opened); per-body bound derived from the measured 1.61 us step for 8 planets
- Time-warp precedent: stock KSP's maximum warp is 100,000x. Principia keeps full n-body integration under warp and reports it smooth at 100,000x with 20 vessels, 'janky but usable' at 1,000,000x and smooth at 6,000,000x with no vessels (desktop); its eclipse predictions lose 'a few minutes of accuracy after a century or so'. — [high] Principia FAQ and Change-Log wiki pages via raw.githubusercontent.com (opened)
- State that can be advanced analytically without breaking conservation: two-body orbits; torque-free rigid rotation (closed form in Jacobi elliptic functions, conserving angular momentum and kinetic energy); lumped thermal relaxation (exponential for linear exchange, closed form for pure radiation); constant-load battery discharge. KSP freezes vessels under on-rails warp into Kepler points with no forces, torques or part physics. Contact, atmospheric flight and close encounters cannot be put on rails. — [medium, unverified] memory (KSP on-rails behaviour and the closed-form solutions); conservation properties of torque-free rotation checked at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poinsot%27s_ellipsoid
- Hierarchical (block) timesteps place bodies on power-of-two rungs dt_n = dt_0/2^n and let them change rung only at synchronisation points. Variable steps break the symplectic property of leapfrog, but time-symmetric variants recover good long-term energy behaviour; the 4th-order Hermite scheme is the standard for collisional n-body work. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1082 (Dehnen & Read review, PDF opened, section 2.3)
- Barnes-Hut trees give O(N log N) but do not pay at solar-system counts: for N = 100 at opening angle 1 the tree needs 902 cell-particle plus 306 particle-particle interactions against 4,950 direct pairs, about 4x fewer at reduced accuracy. Few-body steps cost microseconds, so the WHFast512 authors and Principia both conclude that parallelising them across threads is counter-productive. — [high] https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1082 (Fig. 5) ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05683 (introduction) ; Principia FAQ ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hannorein/rebound/main/docs/gravity.md (all opened)

### Implications for the design
- Add a celestial tier above the mechanism tier: CPU, float64, single-threaded, with every body stored relative to its parent in a frame tree (galaxy, star system, planet, moon, local workshop). The existing workshop design becomes the leaf frame and never sees absolute coordinates.
- The mechanism and matter tiers must run in a local frame that is re-based in both position and velocity (floating origin plus velocity re-basing). Float32 is acceptable only within about 4-8 km of the local origin; the fp32-only GPU can receive gravity only as a uniform vector plus an optional 3x3 tidal-gradient matrix computed on the CPU in float64.
- Uniform g stops being a constant and becomes a derived, declared approximation: g_eff = GM/R^2 minus the centrifugal term, with Coriolis, Euler and tidal terms reported as neglected or enabled. Their sizes are known (Coriolis 1.5e-4 g at 10 m/s, gradient 3 ppm per 10 m, lunar tide 1.1e-7 g), so the Reality Report can list them, and fictitious-force work needs its own ledger account distinct from numerical loss.
- Give each gravitating body one of three declared propagation modes: on-rails Kepler (exact two-body, cost independent of warp), symplectic n-body (Wisdom-Holman or a high-order multistep; bounded energy error, cost linear in warp), and a high-order adaptive reference (IAS15 class) used for close encounters and as the test oracle.
- Do not use plain leapfrog for planetary or moon orbits: its along-track error is about 155,000 km per year at 100 steps per orbit. Use Kepler-splitting or rails there and keep leapfrog-type stepping for the local 480 Hz physics.
- Time warp should be a ladder with a declared fidelity at each rung: full physics at low warp; mechanisms frozen into rigid composites with point-mass orbit and analytic attitude at medium warp; 34-body n-body up to about 6e5x; planets-only Wisdom-Holman up to about 8e9x; pure rails beyond. The gravity budget is 3% of one core, so the thermal governor is unaffected.
- Bit-exact replay extends to the celestial tier only if the Kepler solver avoids library sin/cos/pow and uses fixed iteration counts with its own series (as WHFast512 does), and fused multiply-add contraction is pinned identically on phone, browser and build server.
- Tree codes, GPU n-body and multithreading are unnecessary for a solar system (up to about 400 direct-sum bodies fit the budget). Rings, asteroid belts and debris should be massless on-rails particles, optionally evaluated on the GPU from orbital elements relative to their parent.
- Planet-scale environment should be analytic fields evaluated on demand, each registered as a declared model with a stated error: layered hydrostatic atmosphere, inverse-square stellar flux, radiative equilibrium (no greenhouse: -33 K for Earth), Sutton-Graves heating (about +/-20%), J2-only gravity (omitting it costs about 590 km/day cross-track in low Earth orbit).
- The charter's out-of-scope list can gain numeric entries: relativity (Mercury 43 arcsec/century; valid for speeds below 0.01c and GM/(r c^2) below 1e-8), finite light-time (499 s per AU), and long-term predictability beyond about 10 Myr.
- Ship only initial state vectors and GM values for about 34 bodies (a few kB) and integrate on device; keep the 32 MB de440s file off the device as a CI oracle for tests such as position error against DE440 after 1, 10 and 100 years.
- 'Or even more' means more hierarchy levels, not a bigger solver: int64 millimetres covers one star system to 0.97 ly, a galaxy needs a star index plus offset or 128-bit positions, stars stay on rails, and only one star system needs to be awake for n-body at a time (the same sleeping idea the plan already uses).

## Multi-scale representation, level of detail, persistence and precedent: how an engine that can afford litres of detailed matter can contain a planet, solar system or galaxy

Every shipped precedent and the numerical literature point to the same architecture: a tree of reference frames (galaxy integer grid or fixed point, star-system f64, body-fixed f64, small f32 region co-moving with the player), closed-form or pre-integrated motion for everything unobserved, a deterministic generator plus a journal of deltas for content, and conservative hand-offs between levels. The existing workshop design becomes the innermost region; what must be added is an orbital/aggregate tier, a frame tree, a generator-plus-journal world store and a hand-off contract that restricts to the same conserved quantities at every level. The documented failures in shipped games are almost all at hand-offs (orbit shifts when entering time warp, docking that does not conserve momentum, mined mass restored on reload, terrain edits silently reverting), which is exactly where a first-principles engine has to be stricter than its precedents.

Out of reach on a phone (declare, do not fake): particle-resolution matter beyond the awake region (Earth is 3.6e22 times the 30 L budget, DERIVED); global oceans, atmospheres, weather and climate as fluid dynamics; planetary collisions and hypervelocity impacts (closing speeds above material sound speed break the rigid/incompressible assumptions); continuous simulation of unobserved regions; exact retroactive evaluation of chaotic unobserved matter (piles, splashes); mutual N-body dynamics of a galaxy or feedback of vessels on planets; time warp with workshop-tier matter awake; two full-budget detail regions at once under about 4 W; storing rather than generating a galaxy (4e11 systems at 1 byte each is 400 GB, DERIVED); relativity and light-time; first-principles formation of star systems at run time.

### Implications for the design
- Frame tree instead of one world space: galaxy level as integer grid or fixed point; each star system in barycentric f64 metres (about 1 mm resolution out to 30 AU, DERIVED); each body in a body-fixed f64 frame; each awake region in a local f32 frame. A single f64 frame must not span more than one star system (2 m resolution at 1 light-year, DERIVED). No absolute position is ever stored in f32.
- Keep an awake region small: within about 500 m of its origin f32 resolves about 0.03 mm (MEASURED, Godot table), which suits a 480 Hz mechanism solver; treat a few kilometres as the hard cap (Jolt's 5 km guidance, Space Engineers' 10 km cluster half-size gives about 1 mm).
- The region frame is itself a physical object with f64 position and velocity (and rotation on a planet surface). The solvers see only velocities relative to it (KSP re-bases velocity at about 750 m/s), and the frame's tidal, Coriolis and centrifugal terms are applied as explicit body forces and entered in the ledger. This is how a vehicle interior or a region at orbital speed stays inside the existing workshop tiers.
- Add an orbital/aggregate tier beneath the mechanism tier: a whole vehicle or settled body is one record (mass by material, centre of mass, momentum, angular momentum, inertia tensor, internal energy, inventory) propagated in closed form or with a symplectic/high-order integrator. Massive bodies follow a deterministic ephemeris computed as a function of time (analytic or integrated ahead of time, as Principia does); vessels are test particles under summed gravity. Both are declared approximations on the fidelity ladder.
- Prefer summed gravity from an ephemeris over patched-conic sphere-of-influence switching: it removes a discontinuous hand-off, costs little (ESTIMATE: 30 bodies x 1,000 vessels x 60 Hz = 1.8 million force evaluations per second) and supports time warp because ephemeris state is a pure function of time.
- One hand-off contract for every level: each representation must restrict to the same conserved record (mass per material, linear and angular momentum, kinetic and internal energy, volume), and promotion must satisfy restrict(promote(c)) == c bit-exactly on the CPU. Kinetic energy lost when coarsening is booked to internal energy in a new ledger account (it is always >= 0, DERIVED), never dropped.
- Hand off only at quiescent states (asleep, in equilibrium, no contact with still-awake matter) and use hysteresis between promote and demote radii, as KSP does with separate load/unload and pack/unpack distances. Where a boundary must cut live matter, treat it like an AMR coarse-fine interface and account for the flux mismatch explicitly.
- World = versioned deterministic generator (CPU, bit-exact, seeded) + journal of deltas. Store only what the player changed (edited voxel leaves, moved bodies, inventories); compact old deltas into coarser conservative summaries instead of evicting them. Silent reversion (No Man's Sky reports) or reset on reload (KSP asteroid mass bug) would break the mass ledger and must be impossible by construction; on quota exhaustion, refuse or coarsen and report it.
- No background simulation of unobserved regions. Classify every process as (a) closed form in elapsed time (orbits, conduction toward equilibrium, battery self-discharge), evaluated lazily from a stored tick as Luanti does with dtime_s; (b) relaxing to an equilibrium that needs no history (liquid to hydrostatic level), jumped to with the dissipated energy booked as heat; or (c) chaotic and history-dependent, frozen and declared as such.
- Time warp is legal only when no workshop-tier matter is awake; requesting warp demotes everything through the conservative hand-off first, and the warp factor is part of the replay log. Use integer ticks (i64 at 480 Hz spans 6.1e8 years, DERIVED) rather than f64 seconds as the master clock.
- The thermal budget is global, not per region: two vehicles far apart share the same roughly 4 W envelope from the existing plan, so the governor's 'amount of awake matter' becomes an allocation across regions, with non-controlled regions pushed to the aggregate tier first.
- Planets are data derived from laws: a radial table from integrating hydrostatic equilibrium with material equations of state (Earth's reference model is 94 rows, well under 10 kB), giving g(r), P(r), density and moment of inertia; terrain and regolith as layered columns of database materials sampled deterministically on demand; atmosphere from scale height. Each generated parameter is labelled catalogue (measured), derived from law, or sampled from a distribution (artistic or statistical).
- Region merge and docking: before contact, move both regions into one frame at their common centre of mass (Space Engineers unions overlapping clusters and re-splits), and test momentum conservation across docking in CI because the reference game shipped without it. Swept collision tests are needed whenever relative motion exceeds a cell per substep (20.8 m per substep at 10 km/s, DERIVED); impacts faster than the material sound speed need a separate declared coarse model.
- Storage design for iOS: append-only journal in OPFS through a synchronous access handle in a worker, navigator.storage.persist() requested, tab-kill-safe, with periodic compaction; assume Safari may evict after seven days without interaction when not installed to the Home Screen, and treat QuotaExceededError as a normal, reported state.
- The lesson from Outer Wilds versus KSP: simulating a whole system continuously works only at miniature scale; at real scale every precedent uses rails plus a small physics region plus time warp. The engine should say which of the two a game has chosen rather than promise both.

### Open questions
- What does deterministic generation cost on an iPhone 15 in single-threaded WebAssembly (for example one 32^3 block of layered terrain materials from a seed), and therefore how fast can the awake region move over a surface inside a 1-2 ms per-frame budget? No measurement was found; any figure today is an ESTIMATE.
- How large are the conservation errors and visible pops on a demote-then-promote round trip of a pile, a liquid volume and a jointed machine, and how much kinetic energy lands in the 'unresolved to internal' account each time?
- Does a Home Screen web app on iOS 26 actually get multi-gigabyte OPFS writes and a granted persist(), and what is the sustained write throughput of a synchronous access handle in a worker? The 60% figure is a documented ceiling, not a measurement on this phone.
- How fast does the delta journal grow in real play (bytes per minute of digging, building and moving objects), and what compaction ratio do conservative summaries achieve?
- Can the mechanism tier at 480 Hz in f32 resolve the fictitious forces of its own frame (Earth-surface Coriolis is about 1.5e-4 m/s^2 per m/s, orbital tidal terms about 6e-5 m/s^2 at 15 m, DERIVED), or do they vanish in rounding and need accumulation in f64?
- Is a bit-exact generator achievable across the phone, the browser and the build server when it needs square roots, logarithms and trigonometry in f64, or must it be written in integer or fixed-point arithmetic with its own math library?
- How should the thermal budget be split when two regions are awake at once, and what is the smallest useful region (bodies and particles) that still feels alive?
- Which ephemeris form is best on the phone: analytic Kepler elements, or a pre-integrated N-body table (size, accuracy over decades, evaluation cost), and do players need effects only N-body gives, such as Lagrange points?
- What happens during atmospheric entry or a landing approach, when the region moves about 128 m per frame at orbital speed (DERIVED from 7.67 km/s at 60 Hz) and terrain must be generated ahead of it?
- Can a second WebAssembly thread be used for background generation on the chosen host (cross-origin isolation was flagged as unavailable on GitHub Pages in the earlier research), or must generation be time-sliced on the physics thread?
- The developer accounts for Outer Wilds, Star Citizen, KSP2, Noita, Universe Sandbox, Elite Dangerous and the Carlson-Hodgins and Chenney papers could not be opened in this session; their details (chunk sizes, zone mechanics, switching criteria, reported speedups) should be re-checked against the primary sources before any design decision leans on them.
